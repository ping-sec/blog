Anything can be a home lab!
Don't get too caught up in completionist syndrome or trying to make the costliest rig of all time. A home lab can be what I started with, a couple of raspberry pi's. I should have spent my money on better gear to start with but my mistakes can be your gain. I'd lean away from Raspberry Pi and other SBC's (Single Board Computers). I'd say that looking towards whatever you have on hand is more cost effective in the beginning. After I brought my own network down several times during prime streaming hours, the family decided I should get more dedicated machines.

I would be mistaken if I didn't mention you can do a lot with virtualization with today's equipment. Most everything made within the last 10 years or longer will have virtualization built-in but verify before you make a large purchase. I can recommend

If you want to start with Raspberry Pi's to get a little taste of Linux, start with the kit from pishop.us, it will cost you $120 USD before any taxes or shipping. The usual note about Raspberry Pi's being overpriced for underperforming comes in true. Shift to something like an Intel NUC or a small form factor like the Lenovo M710s. After you get tired of those, consider something like a Dell or HP Server. I was lucky enough to find a local marketplace seller that happened to be giving away their home lab. I ended up with 2 Dell R720's and 1 Dell R610, plus some handy Cisco switches.
